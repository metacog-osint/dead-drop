How this is worked out

Each unit is stored as its size in SI units, and a value is converted by multiplying by the size of the unit it is in and dividing by the size of the unit wanted. The sizes are the definitions in NIST Special Publication 811, appendix B, most of them exact: the foot is 0.3048 m, the statute mile 1,609.344 m, the nautical mile 1,852 m, the pound 0.45359237 kg, the pound-force 0.45359237 kg × 9.80665 m/s², the standard atmosphere 101,325 Pa. The millimetre and inch of mercury are NIST's conventional values, 133.3224 Pa and 3,386.389 Pa. The astronomical unit is the IAU's 2012 definition, 149,597,870,700 m. Temperatures are converted through kelvin (°C = K − 273.15, °F = 1.8 × °C + 32, °R = 1.8 × K). The mil shown is the NATO mil, 6,400 to a circle; other armies have used 6,000 or 6,300.

Mach number. The speed of sound depends on the air's temperature only: a = √(γ R T), with γ = 1.4 and R = 287.05287 J/(kg·K) for dry air, so 340.29 m/s (1,225.06 km/h) at the standard sea-level 15 °C and 295.07 m/s in the −56.5 °C air from 11 to 20 km. The standard temperatures are those of the U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976, the same as the ICAO standard atmosphere up to 32 km. Mach 1 is not one fixed speed, which is why the tool asks for the temperature.

Flight level is pressure altitude in hundreds of feet: the height the standard atmosphere gives for the pressure measured with the altimeter set to 1013.25 hPa (29.92 inHg). It matches the true height above sea level only when the day's air matches the standard atmosphere, so it cannot be worked out from a geometric altitude. Levels are rounded to the nearest whole level; in practice aircraft are assigned levels in steps of 10.

References: NIST Special Publication 811 (2008), Guide for the Use of the International System of Units, appendix B; IAU 2012 resolution B2; U.S. Standard Atmosphere, 1976. Test: tests/unit-converter.test.js (1 km = 0.6213712 mi; 1 atm = 14.695949 psi; Mach 1 at sea level = 340.294 m/s).
