Satellite Conjunction Analysis

Screens two objects in orbit for close approaches (conjunctions) in a time window: each time they pass nearest each other, the time of closest approach (TCA), the miss distance between them then, and how fast they pass. It is a first look from public element sets, not a collision risk: the miss distance carries an error of a kilometre or more.

Closest approaches

Times in your chosen zone (the Time button). Radial, along-track and cross-track are the parts of the miss along object A's position from the Earth's centre, along its direction of travel, and square to its orbit plane.

How this is worked out

The SGP4 model (satellite.js 5.0.0, MIT licence) moves both element sets through the window. The separation between the two is sampled at the search step; every sample lower than both its neighbours brackets a closest approach, which a golden-section search then narrows to a millisecond. Sampling alone is not enough: two low orbits crossing at 10 km/s move 300 km relative to each other in 30 seconds, so the nearest sample can be hundreds of kilometres from the true miss distance. At each closest approach the page gives the miss distance, the relative speed, the angle between the two directions of travel (near 180° is head-on), and the miss split into object A's radial, along-track and cross-track directions. Where the two are moving apart at the start of the window, or closing at its end, the nearest approach lies outside the window and is reported apart.

Limits. Element sets and SGP4 place an object to within about a kilometre at the set's epoch, and the error grows by one to a few kilometres a day, mostly along the track (a rough guide, worse for low objects in a strong atmosphere and for objects that manoeuvre). So a miss distance from this page is a screening figure with kilometre-level error, good for deciding which pairs deserve a closer look, not for judging whether they will collide. Operators judge that from a probability of collision, worked out from both objects' position covariance (their uncertainty, stated as an ellipsoid) and combined size, as carried in a Conjunction Data Message from a tracking service; this page cannot give one. The screening distance is your choice; it is not a safety threshold. No network service is used beyond loading the page.

References: Alfano and Negron, "Determining satellite close approaches", Journal of the Astronautical Sciences 41(2), 1993; Press et al., Numerical Recipes, 3rd ed., section 10.2 (golden-section search); Vallado, Fundamentals of Astrodynamics and Applications, 4th ed., section 3.3 (the radial, along-track, cross-track frame). Test: tests/conjunction.test.js (two circular orbits 500 km up crossing at right angles, 0.01 rad apart: miss 48.635 km at 2,833.887 s, passing at 10.766 km/s; a straight-line pass that sampling at 60 s puts at 100.1 km and refinement finds at 5 km).
