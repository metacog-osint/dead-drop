Geography and mapping

Bearing and Distance Overlay

Draws range rings (every point a set distance from a centre, such as a sensor's reach) and bearing lines (the shortest path leaving a point in a set compass direction, such as a line of bearing from a direction finder) on a map. Both are drawn as they really lie on the Earth, so long lines curve and large rings stretch on the flat map, and each line's end point and arrival direction are listed.

Centre

Decimal degrees: north and east positive. New shapes start from this centre.

Range ring

Distance over the surface from the centre; under 20,015 km (half way round the Earth).

Bearing line

Bearing is degrees clockwise from true north (not magnetic north) at the centre.

Shapes on the map

How this is worked out

The Earth is treated as a sphere of radius 6,371 km. Every vertex of a range ring, and every point along a bearing line, is the destination point: where you arrive after travelling a distance d along a great circle that sets off from latitude φ1, longitude λ1 on bearing θ,

φ2 = asin(sin φ1 cos δ + cos φ1 sin δ cos θ)    λ2 = λ1 + atan2(sin θ sin δ cos φ1, cos δ − sin φ1 sin φ2)

with δ = d / R. A ring uses 180 vertices; a line uses one point every 50 km or so, so the drawing follows the true path instead of a straight line on the flat map. The arrival bearing is the direction of travel at the end of the line; it differs from the starting bearing on all but north-south lines and the equator, and the arrow at the end of each line points that way. A ring that contains a pole is closed through the pole.

Limits. A sphere, not the WGS 84 ellipsoid: positions can differ from an ellipsoidal calculation by up to about 0.5% of the distance. A bearing line is a great circle; a ship or aircraft holding a constant compass heading follows a rhumb line instead, which differs over long distances. The map uses the Web Mercator projection, which stretches shapes toward the poles. Map tiles come from OpenStreetMap (tile.openstreetmap.org); no other network service is used, and the shapes are kept only in this browser.

Reference: Chris Veness, "Calculate distance, bearing and more between latitude/longitude points", movable-type.co.uk (destination point). Test: tests/bearing-overlay.test.js (from 53° 19′ 14″ N, 001° 43′ 47″ W on 096° 01′ 18″ for 124.8 km: 53° 11′ 18″ N, 000° 08′ 00″ E).
