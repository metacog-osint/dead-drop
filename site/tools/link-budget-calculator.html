Link Budget Calculator

Adds up the gains and losses between a radio transmitter and a receiver to give the power that arrives, and how far that power sits above the weakest signal the receiver can use (its sensitivity). The difference is the link margin: positive means the link should close, negative means it should not.

Transmitter

Decibels relative to one milliwatt: 30 dBm is 1 W, 40 dBm is 10 W.
Gain over an ideal antenna that radiates equally in every direction.

Receiver

The weakest signal the receiver decodes at its rated bandwidth and error rate, from its data sheet.

Path

Straight-line distance; for a satellite, the slant range.
Your own allowance for atmospheric gases, rain, polarisation mismatch and pointing error.

How this is worked out

The effective isotropic radiated power (EIRP) is the transmit power plus the antenna gain minus the cable loss. The free-space path loss is the spreading of the wave over distance d at wavelength λ:

Lfs = 20 log₁₀(4π d / λ) = 32.45 + 20 log₁₀ f(MHz) + 20 log₁₀ d(km) dB

The received power is EIRP − Lfs − other losses + receive antenna gain − receive cable loss, and the margin is the received power minus the sensitivity. The range at zero margin follows because free-space loss grows by 20 dB for every tenfold increase in distance: d × 10margin/20.

The margin wording is a rule of thumb, not a standard: below 0 dB the signal is under the stated sensitivity; under 6 dB leaves little room for fading and pointing error; 6 dB or more is a margin often planned for. Fast-fading or low-elevation links are usually planned with more.

Limits. Free-space loss assumes a clear line of sight with nothing in or near the path. Over the ground, reflections, terrain, buildings and the curve of the Earth usually add a great deal more loss; ITU-R P.452, P.526 and P.1546 cover those cases. The answer is only as good as the sensitivity figure, which holds for one bandwidth and modulation. Antennas are assumed to be pointed at each other and in each other's far field.

Reference: ITU-R Recommendation P.525, Calculation of free-space attenuation, section 2.2 (the 32.45 dB constant is computed from the speed of light as 32.448 dB; P.525 rounds it to 32.4); Sklar, Digital Communications, 2nd ed., chapter 5 (link budget analysis). Test: tests/link-budget.test.js (437.5 MHz over 2,000 km gives 151.29 dB free-space loss).
