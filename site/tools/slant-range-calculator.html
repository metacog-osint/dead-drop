Slant Range Calculator

The straight-line distance from an observer to a satellite, from the satellite's altitude and how high it stands above the observer's horizon (its elevation angle), and how long a radio signal takes to cover it.

Height above the Earth's surface. Low Earth orbit is about 300 to 2,000 km; geostationary is 35,786 km.
0 is on the horizon, 90 is straight overhead.
0 for sea level; 1.6 for Denver.

How this is worked out

The Earth is treated as a sphere of radius 6,371 km. With the observer at radius Ro and the satellite at Rs, the angle at the satellite between the observer and straight down (the nadir angle, η) and the angle at the Earth's centre between the two (the central angle, λ) are

sin η = (Ro / Rs) cos(elevation)    λ = 90° − elevation − η

and the slant range follows from the law of cosines, range² = Ro² + Rs² − 2 Ro Rs cos λ. Ground range is the arc λ along the surface. The delay is the range divided by the speed of light in a vacuum; the atmosphere adds a little.

Reference: Wertz and Larson, Space Mission Analysis and Design, 3rd ed., section 5.2. Test: tests/slant-range.test.js (550 km at 45° gives 749.2 km).
