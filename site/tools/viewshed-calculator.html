Viewshed Calculator

Whether an observer, a camera or an antenna can see each of a set of target points: the page fetches the ground height at evenly spaced points along each line, lowers each point for the curve of the Earth, and checks that the straight sight line clears every one. For a blocked target it says where the ground gets in the way and how much higher the target would need to be.

How this is worked out

For each target the page takes evenly spaced points along the great circle (the shortest path over the Earth's surface) from the observer, and asks the Open-Elevation service for the ground height at each. Each point's height is lowered by the curvature drop, d² / (2 k R), where d is its distance from the observer, R is the Earth's mean radius (6,371 km) and k is the refraction factor. The eye is the observer's ground plus the observer's height, and the target top is its ground plus its height, lowered the same way. The target is visible when the straight line between them passes at or above every point in between.

drop = d² / (2 k R)    horizon distance = √(2 k R h)

k = 4/3 is the standard figure for radio waves in an average atmosphere (ITU-R Recommendations P.453 and P.834); it puts the horizon for an eye 2 m up at 5.8 km. For visible light the surveyors' refraction coefficient of about 0.13 gives k of about 7/6. Near the ground both vary widely with the weather, and on a cold, still morning a line can bend far more, so a marginal result, within a few metres, can go either way.

Limits:

  • The ground model has gaps between samples: a ridge or building narrower than the spacing (shown with each result) can be missed. Open-Elevation serves a public digital elevation model built from the SRTM survey; its resolution, and whether a point's height includes trees and buildings, depend on the data the service holds and are not checked here. Treat the result as a first look, not a survey.
  • This is geometric line of sight. It does not include the Fresnel zone (the region around a radio path that must also be clear for full signal), haze, or how small the target looks at that range.
  • Heights are taken as above the same datum as the elevation model (for SRTM, mean sea level, EGM96).

Services used: Open-Elevation (api.open-elevation.com), a free public service, for the ground heights: the page sends it the latitude and longitude of every sample point, and nothing else. If it does not answer, the page says so and calculates nothing. Map tiles come from OpenStreetMap.

References: ITU-R P.453 and P.834 (effective Earth radius, k = 4/3), as used for path profiles in ITU-R P.452 and P.526; Chris Veness, "Calculate distance, bearing and more between latitude/longitude points" (movable-type.co.uk) for the great-circle path. Test: tests/viewshed.test.js (synthetic terrain: a 2 m eye over a smooth Earth sees to 5,829 m; a 10 m ridge 5 km out, with 4/3 Earth, leaves the sight line 9.47 m short and the target needing 18.94 m more height).
