Geography and mapping

Coordinate Converter

Writes one position in the common forms: decimal degrees (DD, such as 28.5721), degrees, minutes and seconds (DMS, 28° 34′ 19.56″), degrees and decimal minutes (DDM, 28° 34.3260′), the UTM grid (Universal Transverse Mercator: a zone number and metres east and north) and the military grid reference (MGRS, which names UTM's 100 km squares with letters). Type latitude and longitude in any of the first three forms, or paste an MGRS reference.

For example 28.6084, -33.8599, 28 36 30.2 N, 28°36'30.2"N or N 28 36.504.
Minus or W for west, E for east.

Or from an MGRS reference

Spaces are optional. The position used is the centre of the square the reference names.

How this is worked out

Degrees, minutes, seconds. One degree is 60 minutes and one minute 60 seconds. The value is rounded once, in its smallest shown unit, before it is split, so a value just under a whole minute carries over (59.999″ shown to two places becomes the next minute, never "60.00″"). Shown precision: DD to 0.000001°, DMS to 0.01″ and DDM to 0.0001′, each about 0.1 to 0.3 m on the ground.

UTM. The transverse Mercator series in Snyder's Map Projections: A Working Manual on the WGS 84 ellipsoid (a = 6,378,137 m, flattening 1/298.257223563), with the UTM conventions: 6° zones, scale 0.9996 on the zone's central meridian, 500,000 m added to eastings and 10,000,000 m to northings south of the equator, and the wider zone 32V over south-west Norway and zones 31X to 37X over Svalbard. The latitude band letter is the MGRS band (C to X in 8° steps from 80° S). UTM is defined from 80° S to 84° N; nearer the poles the polar grid (UPS) is used, which this page does not give. UTM eastings and northings are shown rounded to the nearest metre. The grid scale factor is how much the grid stretches distances at the point (0.9996 on the central meridian, a little over 1 near the zone edges).

MGRS. Built from the UTM position: zone and band, two letters for the 100 km square (columns lettered A–H, J–R or S–Z depending on the zone; rows A–V, skipping I and O, repeating every 2,000 km and starting at F in even-numbered zones), then the metres east and north within that square, shown to 1 m (five digits each way). The digits are cut off, not rounded, so the reference names the square the point is in. An MGRS reference names a square, not a point: a ten-digit reference is a 1 m square, an eight-digit one a 10 m square, and so on. Reading an MGRS reference back uses the open-source mgrs library (version 1.0.0, loaded from cdn.jsdelivr.net), which gives the centre of the square.

Limits. Datum: everything is WGS 84. A position taken from an old paper map on another datum can be off by tens to hundreds of metres. No network service is used apart from loading the mgrs library.

References: J. P. Snyder, Map Projections: A Working Manual, USGS Professional Paper 1395 (1987), pp. 60–64; NGA (formerly DMA) TM 8358.1 and 8358.2 (UTM and MGRS).
