Time

Multi-Time-Zone Converter

Type a date and time as a clock in one place reads it, name that place's time zone, and see the same moment at launch sites, control centres and the common time zones. Daylight saving time (summer time, when clocks move an hour ahead) is applied by each zone's own rules, and a time that a clock change skips or repeats is flagged.

How this is worked out

Each zone's rules come from the IANA time zone database (the "tz" or "Olson" database), which your browser carries and reads through its Intl.DateTimeFormat interface. The page turns the time you type into one instant in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) by finding the zone's offset from UTC in force at that moment, then reads that instant on each zone's clock.

Twice a year, in zones with daylight saving time, a local time can be missing or doubled. When clocks go forward (America/Chicago at 02:00 on 8 March 2026) the hour from 02:00 to 02:59 never happens; the page moves such a time forward by the length of the gap, so 02:30 reads as 03:30 CDT (08:30 UTC). When clocks go back (01:00 to 01:59 on 1 November 2026) the hour happens twice; the page uses the first one, 01:30 CDT (06:30 UTC), and gives the second, 01:30 CST (07:30 UTC). This is the "compatible" rule of RFC 9557 and the TC39 Temporal proposal. The zone abbreviations (CDT, GMT+5) are the browser's; where a zone has no common abbreviation the browser writes its offset instead.

Limits: the answers are only as current as the browser's copy of the tz database, so a clock-rule change announced recently may be missing. Baikonur is shown on Asia/Qyzylorda, the IANA zone for the Kyzylorda region; Kazakhstan moved the whole country to UTC+5 on 1 March 2024, and the leased city's own practice is not checked here. Times before 1970 rest on the database's historical records, which are less certain.

Reference: IANA tz database (tz-link, "Theory and pragmatics of the tz code and data"); ECMA-402 for Intl; RFC 9557 for the treatment of skipped and repeated times. Test: tests/timezone.test.js (America/Chicago on 8 March and 1 November 2026, London, Sydney, Kolkata, the date line).
