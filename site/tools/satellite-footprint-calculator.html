Satellite Footprint Calculator

The footprint is the patch of ground from which a satellite stands at least a chosen height above the horizon (the minimum elevation angle). This works out how wide that patch is, how much of the Earth it covers, how far away the satellite is from its edge, and how wide a beam the satellite needs to reach it.

Height above the Earth's surface. Low Earth orbit is about 200 to 2,000 km; geostationary is 35,786 km.
How high above the horizon the satellite must be to be usable: 0 is the horizon itself; 5 to 10 is common, to clear terrain, buildings and the thick air near the horizon.

How this is worked out

The Earth is treated as a sphere of radius R = 6,371 km, with the satellite at radius R + h. Seen from the satellite, the Earth's disc has angular radius ρ, and the edge of the footprint lies at a nadir angle η (the angle at the satellite between straight down and the edge point) and an Earth central angle λ (the angle at the Earth's centre between the point below the satellite and the edge point):

sin ρ = R / (R + h)    sin η = sin ρ · cos(elevation)    λ = 90° − elevation − η

The footprint radius is the arc R·λ along the surface. The area is the spherical cap 2πR²(1 − cos λ), not a flat circle: for a geostationary satellite at 0° a flat circle of the same radius would overstate it by almost a fifth (257 against 216 million km²). The slant range to the edge is R sin λ / sin η, worked here with the law of cosines, which gives the same number. The nadir angle is the half-width a satellite's antenna or sensor beam needs to reach the footprint edge.

Limits: a sphere, not the WGS 84 ellipsoid (differences of a few tenths of a per cent); no terrain; the footprint of one instant, not the swath swept along the orbit; and the geometry only, not whether a link closes, which also depends on power and antennas.

Reference: Wertz and Larson, Space Mission Analysis and Design, 3rd ed., section 5.2 (Earth geometry viewed from space). Test: tests/satellite-footprint.test.js (550 km at 5° gives a 2,057.6 km footprint radius and 2.585 % of the Earth; geostationary at 0° sees 42.44 %).
