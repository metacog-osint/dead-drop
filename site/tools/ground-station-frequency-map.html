How this is worked out

Band names. Two systems are in use and they overlap, so the tool shows both and never mixes them. The letter bands (L, S, C, X, Ku, Ka and so on) are IEEE Std 521-2019's radar-band letters, which satellite engineers use loosely too. The ITU bands are the Radio Regulations' decade bands: band N runs from 0.3 × 10N to 3 × 10N Hz, so VHF (band 8) is 30–300 MHz, UHF (band 9) 300–3,000 MHz and SHF (band 10) 3–30 GHz. A 1.5 GHz signal is therefore IEEE L-band and ITU UHF at the same time. On an exact edge the ITU rule is used for both systems: the lower limit is excluded and the upper included, so 2 GHz is L and 3 GHz is ITU UHF. Wavelength is the speed of light, 299,792,458 m/s, divided by the frequency.

Space usage differs from the IEEE letters in two places this list shows. The deep-space "X-band" uplink, 7,145–7,190 MHz, lies in IEEE C-band, and the satellite "Ku-band" downlink, 10.7–12.75 GHz, starts in IEEE X-band. The list keeps the space name in each link's description and files the link under the IEEE letters it actually falls in.

The station list. Each entry gives the band each kind of link uses under the ITU Radio Regulations (Article 5): space operation and space research, 2,025–2,110 MHz up and 2,200–2,290 MHz down; Earth exploration-satellite data, 8,025–8,400 MHz down; deep space, 2,110–2,120 and 7,145–7,190 MHz up, 2,290–2,300 and 8,400–8,450 MHz down; NASA's TDRS relay link, 14.6–15.25 GHz up and 13.4–14.05 GHz down; the U.S. military Space-Ground Link Subsystem (SGLS), 1,755–1,850 MHz up and 2,200–2,300 MHz down; fixed-satellite Ku-band, 14.0–14.5 GHz up and 10.7–12.75 GHz down. These are the allocated bands, not the exact channels a station uses on a given day, and a station may also work in bands not listed here (many add Ka-band). Only stations whose purpose and bands their operators state publicly are listed; positions are rounded to 0.01° (about 1 km). It is a reference list, not a monitoring feed.

Services. Map tiles come from OpenStreetMap (tile.openstreetmap.org). Nothing else is fetched; stations you add are kept only in this browser.
