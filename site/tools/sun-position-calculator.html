How this is worked out

The Sun's place is found with the low-accuracy method of Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, chapter 25, the same one the NOAA Solar Calculator uses: from the time, the Sun's mean longitude and mean anomaly, the equation of centre (the correction for Earth's elliptical orbit), then the apparent longitude with small corrections for nutation (the wobble of Earth's axis) and aberration (the shift from Earth's motion). That gives the Sun's declination (its latitude on the sky) and the equation of time (how far a sundial runs ahead of or behind clock time, up to about 16 minutes). The hour angle, how far the Sun is past the local meridian, follows from the time and longitude, and the elevation and azimuth from spherical trigonometry (Meeus chapter 13).

sin(elevation) = sin(lat) sin(dec) + cos(lat) cos(dec) cos(hour angle)

The elevation shown first includes atmospheric refraction, which lifts the Sun's image by about half a degree at the horizon and almost nothing overhead (NOAA's formula, for a standard atmosphere); the geometric elevation without it is shown too. Sunrise and sunset are when the Sun's upper edge meets a level horizon, the centre 0.833° below it (0.567° of refraction plus the Sun's 0.267° radius). Civil, nautical and astronomical twilight end when the centre is 6°, 12° and 18° below the horizon; astronomical twilight's end is the start of full darkness. Each event is iterated on the declination at the event itself.

Accuracy: about 0.01° in the Sun's position (Meeus), and sunrise and sunset to about a minute between 72° S and 72° N (NOAA), more loosely nearer the poles. Real refraction near the horizon changes with temperature and pressure and can move sunrise by a minute or more. The horizon is assumed level and at sea level: hills, and the observer's height, are not taken into account. Times are treated as UTC rather than Terrestrial Time, a difference of about 69 seconds that moves the Sun by under 0.001°.

The page uses no outside service and no library: the maths is in lib/sun-position.js.

References: Meeus, Astronomical Algorithms, 2nd ed., chapters 13, 15, 22, 25 and 28; NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory, Solar Calculator and its "Solar Calculation Details".
