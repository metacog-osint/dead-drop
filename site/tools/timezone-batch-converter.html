Time

Time Zone Batch Converter

Paste timestamps from a log, a report or a spreadsheet, one per line, and see each one as a single moment in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) and in the time zones you tick. A line that carries its own zone (Z, UTC or an offset such as -05:00) is read with it; a line without one is read as a clock in the source zone you name. Lines that cannot be read stay in the table with the reason.

Read: 2025-10-26T14:30:00Z, 2025-10-26 14:30 (no zone), 1729952400 (Unix seconds), 1729952400000 (milliseconds), 26 Oct 2025 14:30 UTC, Oct 26, 2025 2:30 pm, 26/10/2025 14:30, 261430ZOCT25 (date-time group: day, hour, minute, zone letter, month, year).
A date that can only be read one way (26/10/2025) is read that way and noted.

How this is worked out

Each line is matched against a fixed set of formats, strictly, rather than handed to the browser's own date reader, whose results for anything but ISO 8601 differ from browser to browser. A line with a zone (Z, UTC, GMT, an offset like +05:30, or a military zone letter) is fixed by it. A line without one is read as a wall clock in the source zone, using that zone's IANA rules for the date in question, so 14:30 in America/Chicago is 19:30 UTC in October and 20:30 UTC in January.

  • Unix time, seconds since 00:00 UTC on 1 January 1970: 9 to 11 digits are read as seconds, 12 to 14 as milliseconds. A shorter number is refused, since it is more likely a year or a count.
  • Numeric dates with the year last are ambiguous: 03/04/2026 is 3 April in most of the world and 4 March in the US. The page uses the order you choose and notes each line where the other order would also have been a valid date.
  • Date-time groups (DDHHMM, zone letter, month, year) use the military zone letters: A to M (skipping J) are UTC+1 to UTC+12, N to Y are UTC-1 to UTC-12, Z is UTC. J means the writer's local time and is refused, since it has no fixed offset.
  • Zone abbreviations such as EST or CST are refused: CST is US Central, China and Cuba Standard Time. Use an offset, or choose the source zone.
  • Clock changes: a time that a change to or from daylight saving time skips is moved forward by the gap; a time it repeats takes the first of the two moments. Both are flagged, with the other reading.

The span is the time from the earliest to the latest line read. Leap seconds are not counted, as in Unix time.

References: ISO 8601-1:2019; RFC 3339; RFC 5322 section 3.3; the IANA tz database; military time-zone letters as in ACP 121. Test: tests/timestamp-batch.test.js (1729952400 is 2024-10-26T14:20:00Z; 261430ROCT25 is 2025-10-26T19:30Z; clock changes in America/Chicago in 2026).
