How this is worked out

Transfer. A Hohmann transfer is the two-burn route between circular orbits of radius r1 and r2: the first burn puts the spacecraft on an ellipse that just touches both orbits (semi-major axis at = (r1 + r2)/2), the second, half an orbit later, matches the target orbit. Speeds come from the vis-viva equation, v = √(μ(2/r − 1/a)), with μ = 398,600.4418 km³/s². The transfer takes half the ellipse's period, π√(at³/μ).

Inclination change. Turning the orbit plane by Δi costs most where the spacecraft moves fastest. Done alone, it costs 2v sin(Δi/2). Folded into a burn that also changes the speed from va to vb, the burn costs √(va² + vb² − 2vavb cos Δi), which is less than doing the two separately. The table shows doing it alone at the larger orbit, folding all of it into the burn at the larger radius (where the spacecraft is slowest), and the cheapest split between the two burns, found numerically. The results above use the cheapest split.

Propellant. The rocket equation, mfinal = mstart · e−Δv/(Isp g0), with g0 = 9.80665 m/s². "Mass left" is the mass after the burns; it is not the dry mass unless every drop of propellant is used.

Limits: circular orbits only; impulsive burns (a low-thrust electric engine spirals out and needs more delta-v than this); both orbits are assumed to cross on the line where the burns happen, so the inclination difference is the whole plane change (a difference in the orbits' orientation, their RAAN, would add more); no launch, gravity losses, drag or margin. "To the Moon's distance" ignores the Moon's own gravity: its first burn is close to a trans-lunar injection, but its second burn is not a lunar orbit insertion.

References: Vallado, Fundamentals of Astrodynamics and Applications, 4th ed., sections 6.3 (Hohmann transfer, Example 6-1) and 6.4 (plane changes, combined maneuvers and the minimum split); Curtis, Orbital Mechanics for Engineering Students, chapter 6. Test: tests/delta-v.test.js (Vallado's Example 6-1: 2.457 + 1.478 = 3.935 km/s in 5.257 h; with a 28.5° plane change folded into the apogee burn, 4.294 km/s; the best split, about 2.2° at the first burn, 4.270 km/s).
