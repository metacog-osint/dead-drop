Geography and mapping

CSV to Map Plotter

Puts many points on a map at once from rows of CSV (comma-separated values, as a spreadsheet exports), and gives the distance and direction of each leg from one row to the next along the shortest path over the Earth's surface.

One point per row. Either a header row naming the columns (lat or latitude, lon or longitude, and optionally name and description, in any order) or no header and name,lat,lon,description. Commas, semicolons or tabs; quote a field that holds a comma. Coordinates in decimal degrees (north and east positive) or degrees, minutes and seconds such as 28°30'15"N.
Read in this browser; nothing is uploaded.

Legs in row order

How this is worked out

The text is split into rows and fields by the CSV rules of RFC 4180: a field holding the separator, a quote or a line break is wrapped in double quotes, and a quote inside it is written twice. The separator (comma, semicolon or tab) is the one the first row uses most. If the first row names latitude and longitude columns, those are used; otherwise the first pair of neighbouring columns that read as a latitude then a longitude in most rows is used. Degrees, minutes and seconds become decimal degrees as degrees + minutes ÷ 60 + seconds ÷ 3600, negative for south and west. A row whose coordinates cannot be read or are out of range (latitude beyond ±90°, longitude beyond ±180°) is listed with its line number and left off the map.

Each leg's length is the great-circle distance (the shortest path over the surface) from the haversine formula on a sphere of radius 6,371 km; its bearing is the initial direction from the first point toward the second, in degrees clockwise from true north. The line drawn for each leg follows that same great circle, so it bends on the flat map, and a leg across the 180° meridian is drawn the short way.

Limits. A sphere, not the WGS 84 ellipsoid: distances can differ from an ellipsoidal calculation by up to about 0.5%. Without a header the tool assumes latitude comes before longitude; a file written longitude first with no header, where every value is within ±90, cannot be told apart, so add a header row. The total is the length of the path in row order, not the shortest route through the points. The CSV text and settings are kept only in this browser. Network use: OpenStreetMap map tiles (tile.openstreetmap.org) and Leaflet from unpkg.com.

References: RFC 4180, Common Format and MIME Type for Comma-Separated Values (CSV) Files; Chris Veness, "Calculate distance, bearing and more between latitude/longitude points", movable-type.co.uk (haversine distance, initial bearing, intermediate point). Test: tests/csv-map.test.js (50° 03′ 59″ N, 005° 42′ 53″ W to 58° 38′ 38″ N, 003° 04′ 12″ W: 968.9 km on an initial bearing of 009° 07′ 11″).
