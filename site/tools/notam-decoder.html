NOTAM Decoder

A NOTAM (notice to air missions) tells pilots and operators about a change or hazard that matters to flying: a closed runway, a navigation aid out of service, rocket or missile firing, a drone operation. This page takes a NOTAM you paste in the international ICAO format and spells out each part in plain words: who issued it, what it is about and what state it describes (from its five-letter Q code), where and between which heights it applies, and when. It draws the NOTAM's circle and any positions or areas in its text on a map. It decodes only the text you give it: it is not a source of current NOTAMs and must not be used for flight planning.

How this is worked out

The format. An ICAO-format NOTAM opens with its series letter, number and year (A1234/26) and its type: NOTAMN (new), NOTAMR (replaces the NOTAM it names) or NOTAMC (cancels the NOTAM it names). Then come the lettered items:

  • Q) the qualifier line, eight fields separated by "/": the flight information region (FIR, the airspace an air traffic unit is responsible for), the NOTAM Code, the traffic it concerns (I for flights under instrument flight rules, V for visual flight rules, IV for both), its purpose (N immediate attention, B pre-flight bulletins, O significant for operations, M miscellaneous), its scope (A aerodrome, E en route, W navigation warning), the lower and upper limits as three-digit flight levels (hundreds of feet on the standard pressure setting; 000 and 999 mean no limit), and a centre and radius: latitude and longitude to the whole minute and a radius in nautical miles (1 NM = 1,852 m).
  • The NOTAM Code is Q plus four letters: the second and third give the subject (for example RD, danger area) and the fourth and fifth its condition (for example CA, activated). XX means the subject or condition is in plain language in item E, and TT marks a trigger NOTAM that points to a published amendment.
  • A) the aerodrome or FIR it applies to (four-letter ICAO location indicators); B) start and C) end, each as YYMMDDhhmm in UTC. "EST" after C means the end time is an estimate and another NOTAM will replace or cancel this one; "PERM" means a permanent change, which the state later puts into its aeronautical information publication (AIP) before cancelling the NOTAM.
  • D) a schedule within B to C, if any; E) the text; F) and G) the lower and upper limits in the units the issuer chose (SFC surface, GND ground, UNL unlimited, feet or metres above mean sea level or ground level, or a flight level). Where F and G are given they are the limits; the Q-line levels are rounded for automatic filtering.

Checks. The page reports each field it cannot read, with the reason, rather than guessing: a Q line without eight fields, an unknown letter, lower above upper, minutes of 60 or more, a date that does not exist, C not after B, a NOTAMR or NOTAMC that names no NOTAM. A Q code not in the table is reported as unknown, since states also use local codes. It also warns when a shape in item E reaches more than 1 NM beyond the Q-line circle (the Q centre is rounded to the minute, up to about 0.7 NM). "In force" compares B and C with your computer's clock and does not read item D's schedule.

Geometry in item E. Coordinates are recognised in the forms 4530N01230E (degrees and minutes), 453012N0123045E (degrees, minutes and seconds), 4530.50N01230.25E (decimal minutes), decimal seconds, and hemisphere-first N453012E0123045. Coordinates joined only by dashes, commas, spaces or "TO" form a run: three or more make an area (closed back to the first point), two a line. A single coordinate with a radius in the same sentence ("5NM RADIUS OF", "RADIUS 10KM", "WI 3NM OF") is drawn as a circle; otherwise as a point. Circles are drawn as true great circles on a sphere of radius 6,371 km (destination-point formula, Veness).

Limits. Boundaries that follow a border, a coastline or an arc ("THEN ALONG THE FIR BOUNDARY", "CLOCKWISE ARC") are not drawn; the page draws straight segments between the listed points, which can differ from the real boundary. Areas that cross the 180° meridian are not handled. Several areas in one text are separated only by the words between them, so check every shape against item E. US domestic-format NOTAMs (starting with "!") are a different format and are refused. The abbreviation list is a selection. This page decodes text; it does not fetch, check or update NOTAMs, and nothing it shows is an official briefing. For flying, use an official NOTAM briefing service.
